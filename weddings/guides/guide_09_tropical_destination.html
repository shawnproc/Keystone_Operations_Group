Getting Started
Your Wedding
Timeline

A destination wedding takes a little extra runway — travel logistics, guest accommodations, and local vendor research all need more lead time. Use this guide to stay organized and keep the magic front and center.

12+ Months
Set Your Vision & Budget
Choose your destination and define your budget — destination weddings range widely. Begin researching local venues and destination wedding planners.
10–12 Months
Book Venue & Local Vendors
Secure your venue and local coordinator first. Book your photographer early — many destination photographers fill up 12–18 months out.
8–10 Months
Send Save-the-Dates
Guests need extra notice for travel planning. Send save-the-dates with flight information, hotel blocks, and your wedding website.
6–8 Months
Attire, Registry & Invitations
Begin attire shopping. Create your registry. Send formal invitations with detailed travel and accommodation instructions.
4–6 Months
Travel & Logistics
Book group travel if applicable. Arrange airport transfers and shuttles. Confirm all local vendor details and review destination-specific contracts.
1–3 Months
Final Confirmations
Confirm guest RSVP counts. Finalize seating, menu, and ceremony script. Prepare final vendor payments and gratuity.
1 Week Before
Travel & Rehearsal
Arrive early to handle any last-minute logistics. Hold your rehearsal dinner. Soak in the destination and breathe.

Insider Advice
Pro Tips & Notes
Destination Wedding Secrets
1
Hire a local coordinator — they know the vendors, the weather patterns, and the logistical quirks of your venue that you never could from afar.
2
Build in a buffer day before the wedding. Travel delays happen — arriving a day early protects the whole event.
3
Check local marriage license requirements well in advance — some destinations require documents 3+ months ahead of time.
4
Plan welcome activities for guests who arrive early — a group dinner or excursion makes the destination feel like an event, not just a ceremony.
5
Get travel insurance for yourself and encourage guests to do the same. Flight cancellations, hurricanes, and the unexpected are real risks.
6
Your photographer MUST visit (or have previously worked at) the venue — golden hour and shade locations change everything in unfamiliar settings.
Questions to Ask Every Vendor
→
Are you available on our date, and are you the person who will actually be there?
→
What is your cancellation and refund policy — especially for weather events?
→
Do you carry liability insurance?
→
What happens if you have an emergency and cannot make it?
→
Can we see references from destination couples you have worked with?
→
What is included vs. what costs extra? Are travel fees included?
→
Do you have a rain or weather backup plan built into the contract?

# Keystone Operations Group — Growth Strategy

Last Updated: June 2026

---

## Core Business Model

Keystone Operations Group sells automated communication systems to small
service businesses. One-time setup fee + monthly support plan. No contracts,
cancel anytime.

**Target client:** Owner-operator service businesses with 1-5 technicians.
Not large companies with full staff and existing infrastructure.

---

## Target Industries (Summer Priority)

- HVAC — busiest season, missing calls while on job sites
- Pest Control — ant/mosquito/termite season, high call volume
- Plumbing — steady year round, missed calls = lost jobs
- Landscaping — quoting season, proposals falling through the cracks
- Electricians — AC installs, outdoor lighting, summer renovation projects
- Carpet Cleaning

**Sweet spot on Google Maps:** Businesses with 15-50 reviews. Established
enough to have customers, small enough to not have a full team.

---

## How to Find Prospects

- **Warm conversations** — business owners you meet naturally. Once you're
  in the mindset, you'll keep running into them. These are warmer than any
  scrape or cold list.
- Google Maps — search "[industry] [city]" and filter for 15-75 reviews
- Angi / Thumbtack — contractors actively spending money to grow already
  understand the value of more leads
- Facebook local business groups
- Nextdoor — service providers advertising there are almost always owner-operators

### OutScraper (Bulk Prospecting)

OutScraper scrapes Google Maps and exports a spreadsheet with business name,
phone, website, address, and review count — ready to work from.

**Recommended filter settings:**
- Category: [target trade — HVAC, plumbing, pest control, etc.]
- Location: [target city or radius]
- Reviews: 10 to 75
- Has phone: Yes
- Rating: 3.5 to 4.4

The 3.5-4.4 rating sweet spot is key. Businesses rated 4.5+ are usually
already dialed in. Under 3.5 have deeper problems than automation can fix.
The middle is almost always an owner-operator doing decent work with no system.

## Prospect Filter — Who Is and Isn't Your Client

| Signal | What it means |
|--------|--------------|
| Owner answers the phone himself | Your client |
| Voicemail picks up during business hours | Your client |
| 15-75 Google reviews, no recent ones | Your client |
| Has reviews but never responds to them | Your client |
| Slow or no response to contact form | Your client |
| Call center answers, polished hold music | Move on |
| 500+ reviews, multiple locations | Move on |
| Dedicated booking software already running | Move on |
| Full office staff handling leads and follow-ups | Move on |

**The rule:** If they've already solved the problem, don't try to resell
the solution. Find someone who hasn't solved it yet.

## The Full Funnel — An Answer for Every Scenario

**Scenario 1 — They're getting leads but losing some:**
> "You're already busy — but how many leads are slipping through? Missed
> calls, slow follow-ups, no review asks after the job. We plug those holes
> automatically."
→ Sell KOS Monthly Support Plan

**Scenario 2 — They're not getting enough leads:**
> "Before we talk automation, let's get your phone ringing first."
→ Sell rank and rent lead gen site. Once leads flow and volume grows,
they need KOS to handle it.

**Scenario 3 — They already have a full support system:**
→ Move on. Not your client right now.

You have an answer for every scenario except the one where they've already
solved everything — and those businesses are easy to spot and skip.

## The Review Angle

Small businesses don't have few reviews because they do bad work. They have
few reviews because no one ever asked at the right moment.

**Pitch:**
> "Right now you have 14 Google reviews. Your competitor down the street has
> 87. Customers pick based on that number before they ever call either of you.
> We can fix that automatically."

Review generation is available as a $79 à la carte add-on or built into
Growth and Enterprise plans.

## First Client Strategy

**Do one free account first.**

Find a friendly contact in a trade business and offer to set up their
automation system at no charge in exchange for honest feedback and a
testimonial if it works.

**What you get:**
- A real GHL sub-account to build and test flows
- Real data — actual texts sent, actual responses, actual results
- A case study with real numbers to show future clients
- Proof the model works before charging strangers

**Ask upfront:** If the results are real, they agree to give an honest
testimonial and let you use their numbers. No contract — gentleman's agreement.

**Use friends as advisors, not clients:**
> "You run a service business — can I show you what I'm building and tell
> me honestly if this would have solved a problem for you a year ago?"

Strangers invest money. Friends give feedback.

## Revenue Stream 2 — Rank and Rent

### What It Is
Build a website targeting a specific local service niche, rank it on Google
through SEO, then rent the leads or the site to a real local business for
a monthly fee. Once ranked, largely passive income.

### The Model
- Build a neutral, generic site (no real business name — e.g. "NoVA HVAC Pros")
- Rank it through local SEO (see below)
- Rent it to a local business for $500-$2,000/mo
- They receive the leads, you collect the rent
- Eventually sell the site outright as a ranked asset

### Key Rules
- Keep completely separate from Keystone Operations Group brand
- Different business name, domain, phone number, address
- No public connection to KOS — revenue flows to you privately
- KOS stays clean and premium; rank-and-rent sites can be scrappier

### How to Rank a Local Site

**On-Page SEO:**
- Target specific keywords (e.g. "emergency HVAC repair Manassas VA")
- Proper page structure — title tags, headers, meta descriptions
- Fast load speed, mobile-friendly

**Citations (NAP consistency):**
- Submit to 30-50 directories: Google Business Profile, Yelp, BBB, Alignable,
  Chamber of Commerce sites, industry directories
- NAP = Name, Address, Phone — must be identical everywhere
- Tools: BrightLocal, Moz Local, Whitespark

**Backlinks:**
- Local directory listings (baseline)
- Guest posts on small business / industry blogs
- HARO (Connectively) — get quoted as an expert, earn a link
- Complementary business partnerships

**Google Business Profile:**
- Critical for local map pack (top 3 listings above organic results)
- Optimize fully — category, description, photos, posts

### Timeline
- Competitive niches: 3-9 months to rank
- Less competitive markets / smaller cities: faster
- Front end is real work — not passive until it ranks

### Selling the Site
Before handoff:
1. Swap business name to buyer's real name
2. Update phone to their real number
3. Update address to their location
4. Update all citations across directories to their NAP
5. Transfer domain or point to their hosting

**What you're actually selling:** The Google ranking, domain authority,
established citation footprint, and proven lead flow — not the placeholder
brand name.

**Lease-to-own approach:** Rent first, prove value, offer the buyout.
If they've been getting 20 leads a month for 6 months, selling is easy.

Getting Started
Your Wedding
Timeline

Planning a wedding is one of the most meaningful journeys you'll take. This guide walks you through every milestone — from the moment you say yes to the last dance under the stars. Use each section to stay on course.

12+ Months
Set Your Vision & Budget
Define your style, guest count, and total budget. Choose a rough season. Begin your venue search — the best venues book 12–18 months in advance.
10–12 Months
Book Venue & Key Vendors
Secure your venue, photographer, and caterer first. These are the hardest to replace. Begin researching florists and entertainment.
8–10 Months
Attire & Guest List
Begin dress shopping — alterations take 4–6 months. Finalize your guest list and collect addresses for save-the-dates.
6–8 Months
Invitations & Registry
Design and send save-the-dates. Create your gift registry. Book hair and makeup. Confirm all vendor contracts are signed.
4–6 Months
Details & Décor
Finalize floral arrangements, centerpieces, and ceremony décor. Send formal invitations. Plan honeymoon travel.
1–3 Months
Final Confirmations
Follow up with all vendors. Finalize seating chart, ceremony script, and menu. Prepare final payments and gratuity envelopes.
1 Week Before
Rehearsal & Last Checks
Hold your rehearsal dinner. Confirm day-of timeline with all vendors. Delegate tasks to your wedding party. Rest and breathe.

Insider Advice
Pro Tips & Notes
Things Nobody Tells You
1
Always get a day-of coordinator — even if you have a full planner. Someone needs to handle the day so you can be the couple, not the project manager.
2
Buffer your timeline by 30 minutes. Something always runs late — that cushion means your guests never notice.
3
Your photographer needs a shot list. Don't assume they'll know every family combination you want captured.
4
Eat. Seriously. Assign someone to make sure you both actually eat at the reception. It flies by fast.
5
Vendors coordinate better when you introduce them via a group email well before the wedding day.
6
Have a rain plan — even for indoor venues. Weather affects outdoor cocktail hours, exits, and transportation.
